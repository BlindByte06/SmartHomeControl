Smart Home Control

  • Author: Philipp Hasel
  • NVDA compatibility: NVDA 2025.1 and newer
  • License: GNU General Public License, version 2
  • Website and source code

Controls smart home devices directly from NVDA – through a simple menu. On/off, brightness, colour, heating, air purifiers, sensors and more, without the sometimes hard-to-use manufacturer apps.

Signing in happens once, with the credentials of the manufacturer account – no additional server and no background setup required. The credentials are stored encrypted, locally on the computer.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • NVDA + Shift + H: open the smart home menu (device overview)
  • NVDA + Ctrl + Shift + P: announce a summary of all devices - how many there are, how many are switched on, which are offline, and a water alarm. Pressed twice, it opens the list of all devices with their state in a reading window, line by line and readable on the braille display.

The settings dialog is available through the "Settings (Alt+E)" button in the device menu.

Inside the open device menu these keys work as well:

  • Ctrl + H: device history (see below)
  • Ctrl + P: platform status – what had a fault and which devices did not answer (see below)
  • Ctrl + T: repeat the status line
  • Ctrl + F: search for a device - it searches platforms, categories, devices and the outlets of a power strip. The same search again moves on to the next match.
  • Ctrl + B: add the selected device or outlet to the favourites, or remove it
  • F1: help for the selected device, listing what it can do
  • F5: refresh the device list

All commands can be given custom shortcuts, and the defaults can be changed: NVDA menu → Preferences → Input gestures → category "Smart Home Control".

The following commands deliberately have no default gesture and can be assigned there when needed:

  • Announce energy consumption – today's and last 7 days' consumption of the metering plugs in kilowatt hours. The values are preferably read directly from the meter inside the device – which keeps counting even while NVDA is not running. Only if a device does not support this query, background samples are used instead (then marked as "estimated", since they only cover the time NVDA was running).
  • Connection diagnostics – announces the connection state per platform, plus network state and the remaining Netatmo token lifetime. During a fault it also says since when it has lasted and what is missing – for instance "Netatmo: weather station not answering".
  • Open the settings – opens the Smart Home settings directly, without the detour through the device menu.
  • Favorites layer – announce status and switch favorites without opening the menu (see below).

Favorites layer

After the assigned shortcut, the add-on prompts with “Choose a favorite: digit 1 to 9” and waits for a digit.

  • Digits 1 to 9 immediately announce the status of the favorite with that number.
  • The same digit again toggles it – as long as it follows the announcement within a few seconds. Five by default, adjustable from one to thirty in the settings. The window is generous on purpose: a purifier announces mode, fan level, air quality and filter life, and the whole of that should be audible before deciding.
  • The same digit after that window announces the status again and opens a fresh one, so switching is never out of reach – it is two quick presses away. This is what stops a digit pressed by accident long afterwards from switching something off.
  • A different digit announces its status and becomes the remembered one, so "1, 2, 1" switches nothing.
  • 0 reads out which digit belongs to which device.
  • Escape leaves the layer, and so does any other key. After a longer pause it closes by itself.

The split is deliberate: the harmless enquiry happens at once, while the consequential switching requires a second, deliberate press. A mistyped digit therefore only announces something instead of switching a device.

What can be switched

Pressing a digit twice on a device that cannot be switched reports "cannot be switched – adjustable in the device menu".

  • Switchable: Meross plugs, lights and LED strips, aroma diffusers; Levoit air purifiers and tower fans; the Cozytouch hot water heat pump (hot water production on/off).
  • On power strips (MSS425, MSS425E, MSS425F) and double outlets (MSS620, MOP320) either a single outlet or the whole device can become a favorite – each with its own digit. The outlet switches only itself, the device switches all outlets together. For a single outlet, expand the strip in the device menu, select the outlet and press Ctrl+B. This works while the device is offline too – the digit stays the same once it is reachable again.
  • Not switchable: Meross sensors (temperature/humidity, water leak) and Meross hubs; every Netatmo device; the Cosori air fryer, which cannot be switched on and off at all and is operated through a cooking programme instead; Petlibro feeders and water fountains, so that a digit pressed twice can never give a meal. Thermostats and radiator valves can be adjusted but not switched on and off; the weather station and the indoor air monitor are display only. Their status is announced on the first press as usual – they are adjusted in the device menu.

Adding favorites and the digits

In the device menu, select the device and press Ctrl+B – or activate the "Add to favorites" entry. Ctrl+B removes a favorite again as well.

When it is added, the device gets its fixed digit, announced right away, for example "living room lamp: added to favorites, digit 3". In the favorites tab the digit is shown in front of the device name. It stays with the device permanently, even when other favorites are removed. From the tenth favorite on, the digits are used up; those devices remain reachable through the device menu and move up as soon as a digit becomes free.

Supported platforms and devices

The add-on supports four smart home platforms. Each can be enabled individually; only the ones actually in use are needed. Cozytouch/Atlantic is experimental – only a single device model has been tested there so far.

Meross

Sign in with the email address and password of the Meross account. External changes (e.g. switching through the Meross app, Alexa or the physical button on the device) are announced as well.

Plugs and power strips

  • MSS210 – switchable plug (on/off)
  • MSS310 – plug with energy monitoring (power, voltage, current)
  • MSS315 – plug with energy monitoring (power, voltage, current)
  • MSS425, MSS425E, MSS425F – power strips; each mains outlet can be switched individually. The USB ports of these strips form a single shared outlet and can therefore only be switched together. Outlets that have their own name in the Meross app appear under that name.
  • MSS620 – outdoor dual plug; both outlets switchable individually (the button on the device itself always switches both together)
  • MOP320 – outdoor dual plug with energy monitoring; both outlets switchable individually (the button on the device itself always switches both together)

Lights and LED strips

  • MSL320 (LED strip), MSL450, MSL610
  • Other MSL models are detected automatically and get the same functions as far as the respective model supports them: on/off, brightness, RGB colour, colour temperature and white presets.

Aroma diffusers

  • MOD150 – spray control: off, light or strong spray (the light function of the device is not controlled by the add-on)

Hubs and sensors

The sensors connect through a Meross hub. Both hub generations (MSH300 and MSH450) are detected automatically; which hub accepts which sensor is defined by Meross and shown in the Meross app.

Supported sensors:

  • MS100 and MS100F – temperature and humidity sensor
  • MS130 – temperature and humidity sensor with display
  • MS400, MS405 – water leak sensors

Netatmo

Heating control and weather station display through the Netatmo account.

Heating

  • NATherm1 – room thermostat; sold as the "Smart Thermostat" or "Smart Modulating Thermostat"
  • NRV – smart radiator valve; sold as the "Smart Radiator Valves"
  • NAPlug – relay/gateway (bridge, required for NATherm1); sold simply as the "Relay", and supplied with the thermostat

Thermostats and radiator valves are grouped by the rooms assigned in the Netatmo app; the room name is also announced as part of the device name.

Adjustable: target temperature (manual, with selectable duration), the operating modes schedule, away and frost guard, and switching the active heating schedule. Also displayed: measured temperature, current mode (including "maximum" if set through the app), boiler/burner status, active schedule zone, pre-heating (anticipation), battery level, and open window detection (this feature is only available on the NRV smart radiator valve, not on the room thermostat).

Weather and indoor air (display only)

  • NAMain weather station with outdoor, wind, rain and additional indoor modules
  • NHC indoor air quality monitor

Displayed: temperature, humidity, CO₂, noise, air pressure, rain and wind – display only, no control.

VeSync

Sign in with the email address, password and country code of the VeSync account. Several brands sit under that account; supported here are devices by Levoit and Cosori.

Levoit

Air purifiers (Core series)
  • Core200S, Core300S, Core400S, Core500S, Core600S
  • as well as the regional variants of these series that VeSync reports as LAP-C201S, LAP-C202S, LAP-C301S, LAP-C302S, LAP-C401S, LAP-C501S and LAP-C601S – regardless of the country code at the end of the model name (e.g. -WEU, -WUSR, -WJP).

Available functions: on/off, mode (manual and sleep; auto on all models except the Core200S), fan level, display, child lock, auto profile (default/efficient/quiet), air quality, and remaining filter life with a warning at low values. The Core200S (and its variants LAP-C201S/C202S) additionally has a controllable night light (on/off/dim).

Tower fans
  • LTF-F422S series, likewise regardless of the country code (tested: KEU, WUSR, WJP, WUS)

Functions: on/off, mode (normal, auto, turbo, sleep), fan level, oscillation, mute and display.

Cosori

Air fryers
  • CAF-P583S series (Dual Blaze), regardless of the country code

Shown, and a running programme can be stopped. The tree gives the name, what the appliance is doing, whether it is online and, while a programme is loaded, its state (ready to start, cooking, finished, paused), the programme itself, the remaining time and both temperatures – the measured one and the one the programme was set to – in the unit the appliance reports. The on/off of the device list steps aside as soon as the appliance reports a cooking state – it says nothing about whether anything is being cooked.

The end of a programme is announced, as are one that starts running and one that is paused – within fifteen seconds while the device menu is open, within a minute while it is closed, and whether the announcement was set off from here or by somebody standing at the appliance. One switch under Notifications in the settings turns all three off together.

Start programme offers the cooking programmes this appliance has reported. Temperature and duration can be adjusted on the way; they start out at the values the appliance holds for the programme. A change made for one cook stays a one-off – it does not redefine the programme, so the same choice means the same thing next time. Adjusting it on the appliance and loading it again does change it, because then the appliance's own setting has changed.

The programme list fills itself. The add-on carries no list of its own and learns each programme from the appliance: every time one is loaded there it is noted, and it can be chosen from then on. Merely selecting a programme on the appliance is enough; it does not have to be cooked. Until one has been reported, the add-on says so instead of opening an empty list.

Change cooking time adjusts a programme while it runs. It is offered only then: before the programme starts the appliance refuses it, so the tree says so instead of holding out a control that cannot work.

A new time is taken as the whole duration and the countdown starts again from it – measured on the appliance, so “set the time to ten minutes” means ten minutes from that moment.

The temperature cannot be changed while the appliance cooks – it does not accept one then. There is therefore no entry for it, but a line that says so and names where the temperature can be set instead: when a programme is started, or on the appliance’s own buttons. To cook the same thing hotter from here, stop the programme and start it again with the temperature wanted.

Stop programme ends a programme that is running.

Both ask before anything is sent. The question names the programme and, for a start, the temperature and the duration; Cancel is the preselected button.

Starting may not mean heating. Cosori state that their air fryers cannot be switched on remotely, to comply with a safety standard that exists so an appliance cannot begin cooking unattended; temperature and time, however, they say, are adjustable remotely. What this appliance actually accepts is set out above: a time whenever a programme runs, a temperature only as a programme is started. Whether this model begins by itself or waits for its own start button is not established here. The programme state says which it was on the next refresh: cooking, or ready to start, in which case the button on the appliance still has to be pressed. The hard part – choosing the programme, the temperature and the time without reading a touch panel – is done either way.

Preheating is not offered. The command for it is documented, but nothing about it has been confirmed against a real appliance, and this one heats.

In standby the appliance stops measuring and keeps sending the last temperature of the previous cook, unchanged for as long as it stands idle. That reading is left out rather than shown as if it were current.

While the appliance holds its temperature, the measurement wobbles by a few degrees from one reading to the next. The displayed value only follows a change of five degrees or more (nine in Fahrenheit), so the line does not get re-read every fifteen seconds without saying anything new. The climb to the set temperature passes through unhindered, as does the reading at the moment a programme ends.

Other device types in the VeSync account (e.g. plugs, lights or humidifiers) are currently not shown.

Cozytouch / Atlantic (experimental)

Experimental. Only one hot water heat pump has been tested so far. Other Cozytouch device types may be detected or presented incorrectly, and individual functions can stop working if Atlantic changes its cloud interface. Details under Maturity and notes.

Sign in with the email address and password of the Cozytouch/Atlantic account.

  • Hot water heat pump (tested: Austria Email Revolution Evo 3; the exact model is shown in the device menu)

Functions: hot water production on/off, target temperature (including the actual heating target with Eco/boost; the cloud does not provide a measured water temperature for this model), heating mode, boost (including an experimentally adjustable boost duration), away mode with a plannable period, available hot water in percent, and display of today's programmed heating windows, whether the appliance is heating right now, and whether the off-peak tariff applies. The rated capacity (in litres) can be configured in the settings.

"Currently heating" says only that the appliance heats, not with what: the cloud reports the same whether the heat pump or the electric heating element is at work. To find out, read the energy consumption twice, an hour apart - the heat pump alone uses at most 0.7 kWh in an hour, with the heating element it is up to 2.5.

The three heating modes:

  • Manual – heats continuously to the configured target temperature.
  • Eco+ – heats economically with a lowered heating target; the actual heating target is shown in the device entry.
  • Program – heats only within the windows of the time program (up to three per day, e.g. for off-peak hours). The device entry shows today's heating times and lets Enter change them: day, start and end of one window, times as HH:MM. Leaving both times empty deletes the window. Below that is a list of weekdays: tick every day that is to get the same windows, so a program for Monday to Friday is one entry rather than five.

    After writing, the add-on reads the time program back from the appliance and says what really stands there. If the appliance does not take the change – possible, since this write has been tried on a single model so far – it says so instead of reporting success.

Petlibro (experimental)

Experimental. Tested with the Air Smart Feeder (PLAF108) and the Dockstream 2 Cordless water fountain (PLWF116). The integration uses the cloud of the Petlibro app as the community has worked it out; Petlibro can change it at any time. Other Petlibro feeders and fountains are shown with the same functions as far as they report the same values; other products (litter boxes, cameras) only show their status. The device entry carries the model number in brackets, e.g. "Air Smart Feeder (PLAF108)".

Amounts are given in portions (feeder) and millilitres (fountain), whatever unit the Petlibro app is set to: the app converts for display only, and the add-on speaks in what the devices really report.

Feeders

  • Status: whether there is enough food, what was given today in total (planned meals and feedings by hand), the last and the next feeding, battery and power supply. The line "Planned meals today" counts the meals of the feeding plan only.
  • Feed now: Enter opens a list of amounts (at most what the feeder gives at once - 16 portions on the Air Smart Feeder); the last amount chosen is preselected. The command is sent once, never repeated. The add-on then waits for the feeder's own record of the feeding and announces what the feeder reports as given. When no confirmation comes - no connection, or the feeder did not answer the cloud - it says that it is unknown whether food was given. The last feeding in the device entry shows it a little later; it is worth checking there before feeding again.
  • Feeding plan: switches all planned meals on or off.
  • Meals: Enter opens a list with one line per meal (name, time, portions, weekdays when not every day, today's state). A meal can be skipped for today only, switched off, changed (time, portions, weekdays, name, and the meal call on feeders with a speaker - the Air Smart Feeder has none), added or deleted - deleting asks first. All of today's meals can be skipped at once as well. The weekdays are seven check boxes; a new meal starts with none ticked. For a daily meal all seven are ticked; with none ticked it is given once.
  • Desiccant: the reminder to replace the desiccant, see below.
  • Light: the indicator light on or off.

The desiccant. A small sachet of silica gel keeps the dry food in the container dry and crisp and protects it from mould. On the Air Smart Feeder it sits in the holder on the underside of the lid of the food container - not loose among the food. The holder is small: Petlibro names sachets of 0.5 gram, at most 2.8 × 1.6 cm, the kind that comes with shoes or electronics (search term "0.5 gram silica gel packs"). Petlibro's own larger desiccant bags are made for the Granary feeders and do not fit here. Petlibro recommends replacing the sachet every two weeks.

The reminder in the app and in the add-on only counts days on the Petlibro server - it keeps counting while the feeder is switched off and knows nothing about whether a sachet was replaced. From the last reset it counts down the change interval, 14 days on the Air Smart Feeder:

  • "Change in 3 days": the sachet is due in three days.
  • "Change due today": the 14 days run out today.
  • "Overdue by 1 day": the 14 days ran out yesterday, the sachet should have been changed yesterday. The last reset was 15 days ago.
  • "Overdue by 27 days" accordingly: the last reset was 41 days ago.

After replacing the sachet, Enter on the line resets the reminder; a question comes first, with Cancel preselected.

Water fountains

  • Status: water level in percent and millilitres, what was drunk today (and yesterday), battery and charging.
  • Single drinks: Enter on the drinking line opens a list of the day's drinks - time, amount and how long each lasted - for today and the six days before.
  • Water mode: off, constant flow, or - on fountains with a presence sensor such as the Dockstream 2 Cordless - flowing when the sensor notices an animal near or further away. Petlibro fountains without a presence sensor - the Dockstream Smart Fountain (PLWF105), the Dockstream RFID (PLWF305) and the Dockstream 2 with power supply (PLWF106) - offer an interval mode instead of the sensor modes: the water flows for a set time and then pauses for a set time; both times are set in the Petlibro app, and the add-on keeps them. These fountains have not been tested with the add-on. Constant flow is offered on the power supply only, because on its battery the fountain gives water, according to Petlibro, only when the sensor notices an animal. Should constant flow be stored while it runs on the battery, no water flows at all; the device entry then says so. Near is Petlibro's default; far makes the sensor more sensitive, so it reacts to an animal further away.
  • Flow duration: how long the water flows once the sensor noticed an animal - 45, 60, 75 or 90 seconds, as in the Petlibro app. Longer leaves more time to drink, shorter saves the battery. The line is there in the sensor modes only, since the duration belongs to them. It can be changed from firmware 0.3.25 on, according to Petlibro; with older firmware the line only shows the value. On the battery the confirmation can take a few minutes, as with every change there.
  • Water warning: the amount below which the fountain counts its water as low, in millilitres (650 to 3000, as in the app).
  • Filter and cleaning: the two reminders. Each is reset with Enter after the filter change or the cleaning - again with a question first.
  • Light: the indicator light on or off.

On the battery, changes take a while. A Dockstream 2 Cordless running on its battery only wakes now and then. The cloud accepts a change at once but does not hear back from the fountain in time; the change arrives when the fountain wakes, after about two minutes. The add-on says that the change is on its way, watches for it and announces it once the fountain reports it (or, after ten minutes, that it did not arrive).

A favourites digit announces the status of a Petlibro device but never switches it - a digit pressed twice must not give a meal.

Setup

Prerequisite: the devices must have been set up once with the respective manufacturer app (Meross, Netatmo, VeSync, Cozytouch, Petlibro) – the add-on then picks them up from the account.

Open the menu with NVDA + Shift + H. Without a sign-in yet, the settings dialog opens automatically. Enable the platforms in use and enter the credentials – details per platform in the following sections. A platform whose credentials are entered anew is switched on by saving, even without ticking it in the "General" tab first. Finally, in the "General" tab: optionally enable "Automatic login" (the connection is then established at every NVDA start) and save. The devices are loaded and immediately available in the menu – no NVDA restart required.

Credentials that were changed are signed in at the platform before they are saved. If the platform refuses them, nothing is stored: the dialog stays open, says why, and the focus lands back in the password field. The same applies to a later change – a new password reaches the platform even while it is connected, instead of only being noticed at the next NVDA start. Should a sign-in fail anyway – because the password was changed in the manufacturer app, for instance – a question offers to enter the credentials again straight away and opens the settings at the affected tab.

Set up Meross

In the "Meross" tab, enter the email address and password of the Meross account – done.

Set up Netatmo

Netatmo uses a browser sign-in (OAuth2) instead of email/password inside the add-on. Required once:

  1. Create a (free) app on dev.netatmo.com; it provides a client ID and a client secret.
  2. Enter both in the "Netatmo" tab of the add-on.
  3. Register the redirect URI with the Netatmo app. It is http://localhost:PORT/callback, with the port from the field above – details, and what the port is about, are explained in the section Netatmo: redirect URI and port.
  4. Choose "Connect with Netatmo (OAuth2)", sign in in the browser and confirm.

Set up VeSync

In the "VeSync" tab, enter the email address, password and country code of the VeSync account.

Set up Cozytouch / Atlantic (experimental)

In the "Cozytouch" tab, enter the email address and password of the Cozytouch account (the same as in the Cozytouch app). Optional: the rated capacity of the hot water tank in litres, so the available hot water is additionally estimated in litres. The tab is labelled “experimental” in the settings dialog – see Maturity and notes.

Set up Petlibro (experimental)

Important: Petlibro allows only one sign-in per account at a time. Signing in with the add-on signs the Petlibro app on the phone out of the same account, and signing in on the phone ends the add-on's session (the add-on then signs in again by itself, which in turn signs the phone out). The way around it is a second Petlibro account:

  1. Create a second account in the Petlibro app - an account created on the website is not accepted for this access.
  2. In the main account, share the feeders and fountains with the second account, and accept the share in the second account.
  3. In the "Petlibro" tab of the add-on, enter the email address and password of the second account and test the connection.

The second account is best given a password that is used nowhere else. Petlibro takes the password - from the app as from the add-on - as an MD5 checksum without salt. The transfer is encrypted, but should the checksum ever fall into other hands, a weak password is easily recovered from it.

Netatmo: redirect URI and port

What is the redirect URI?

During the browser sign-in, Netatmo sends the authorisation back to a previously registered address – the redirect URI. It follows from the port set in the "Netatmo" tab. By default it is exactly:

http://localhost:8474/callback

Exactly this address has to be entered in the "redirect URI" field of the Netatmo app on dev.netatmo.com – best copied unchanged from the add-on.

Why does the address have to match exactly?

Netatmo compares the registered redirect URI character by character with the one actually used. Even a difference in scheme (http), host (localhost is not the same as 127.0.0.1), port or path leads to the error redirect_uri mismatch.

What is the port for, and why 8474?

During sign-in, the add-on briefly starts a small local web server on the user's own computer that receives the authorisation from Netatmo. The port (default 8474) determines the "channel" this local server listens on. It is only used locally and only for the moment of signing in; nothing is opened to the outside. 8474 was chosen as an inconspicuous, rarely used port.

If the port is already taken on the computer (the sign-in fails with a port message), simply change the redirect port in the Netatmo tab to a free value – and register the newly shown redirect URI with dev.netatmo.com again so both sides match.

Usage

In the device menu the devices are grouped by platform and type in a tree view. Navigate with the arrow keys; switch or change values with Enter or Space. Frequently used devices can be marked as favourites (via "Add to favorites" on a device); they then also appear in the favorites tab. In the settings it can be chosen which tab (all devices or favorites) is shown first when the menu opens.

Every action gives immediate speech and tone feedback. While the menu is open the devices are refreshed more frequently so the values stay up to date.

Device history

Ctrl + H in the device menu opens the history. It answers two different questions, and the "View" combo box switches between them.

Events – one row per action, newest first and grouped by day ("Today", "Yesterday", then weekday and date). The columns are time, device, platform, event and source. The source column names where the action came from: "you" for an action through this add-on, "external" for the manufacturer app, a voice assistant or the button on the device, and "automatic" for entries the add-on records by itself, such as a water alarm, and for what a device does on its own: a Netatmo heating schedule moving to its night zone at 22:00 ("Schedule zone changed"), the heating of a Netatmo thermostat starting or stopping ("Heating active", "Heating off"), an open window the thermostat detects, a boost of the heat pump running out after its duration ("Boost ended"), a filter or a cleaning falling due, a low battery, or a cooking programme of the air fryer running to its end. A temperature set by hand in the Netatmo app is one row with its end time, as one set here.

Measurements – one row per device and quantity instead of thousands of single values: device, quantity, latest value and time of the latest reading. Enter on a row opens the details – lowest and highest value, the time-weighted average, the period covered, the number of stored readings and the most recent changes. The detail window is a read-only text field, so it can be read line by line and copied with Ctrl+C. For the heating of a Netatmo thermostat the details give the share of time it was active instead of lowest, highest and average, and the changes are its switchings.

The filters above the list narrow it down by device, platform and period (all time, last hour, last 24 hours, last 7 days, last 30 days); the "Filter" button applies them.

What is recorded

Readings are kept as change points, not as a complete series: a value is stored when it differs from the last stored one by more than a threshold – 0.3 K for temperature, 2 % for humidity, 50 ppm for CO2, 1 mbar for air pressure, 3 µg/m³ for particulates and 5 dB for noise. Regardless of that, one value per hour is kept, so an unchanged reading does not look like a gap and the averages stay correct. Recorded are temperature, humidity, CO2, air pressure, PM2.5 and PM10 of the Levoit air purifiers, the noise level of the Netatmo indoor module, the temperature measured by the Levoit tower fans and the heating of the Netatmo thermostat, active or off, at every switching. The heating is thus in two places: each switching as an event, and as a series that tells how long it ran.

Events are kept for one year or 2000 entries, readings for 90 days or 20000 change points – whichever limit is reached first. The switchings of the Netatmo heating have a limit of their own, 30 days or 3000 entries: it switches some ten times an hour while it runs and would otherwise push the other events out within weeks. Both files live in the NVDA configuration folder next to the add-on, as SmartHomeControl_history.json and SmartHomeControl_measurements.json. "Delete history" empties both after a confirmation prompt.

CSV export

"Export as CSV" writes what the current view shows, with the active filters. The columns follow the content: a file of events carries no measurement columns, and a file of readings only the quantities that actually occur.

Timestamps are written in ISO 8601 with the "T" (2026-08-14T22:20:49). That is not cosmetic: with a space instead, a spreadsheet recognises a date in it and reformats the cell, instead of leaving the timestamp standing as text. List and decimal separators follow the regional settings, so numbers arrive as numbers rather than being read as dates.

Change announcements

External changes are announced as well – for example when a device is switched through the manufacturer app, Alexa or the button on the device. In the "Notifications" tab, what is announced can be set per platform and event type (e.g. switching, mode, fan level, air quality, filter, thermostat setpoint, boiler status). Own actions in the dialog are not reported twice. A change made elsewhere goes into the history in any case, also with its announcement switched off.

The water sensors are a special case: an MS400 or MS405 reports a state, not a measured value. A change is announced with an error tone and recorded in the history as an event ("water alarm" or "no water detected any more"). Since a leak sensor that stays silent is pointless, this is on by default; it can be switched off under Notifications → Meross → "Announce water alarm of the water sensors".

Faults of a platform have their own section "Faults" in the same tab - separate from the changes below, because a fault is not a change, and the master switch for external changes does not cover it. Four switches: announce at all, no longer reachable, answering only in part, reachable again. All on by default.

A fault is announced only once it has lasted two minutes: the clouds drop out several times a day for less than a minute, and each of those used to interrupt twice - when it failed and when it came back. The return is announced only after a fault that was. The device tree, the status line and the platform status show a fault at once.

Whatever is switched off here is still recorded: the device tree, the status line and the platform status keep showing it. "Off" means "do not interrupt", not "do not keep track".

Petlibro has a section of its own. On by default: failed feedings and faults of a device, feedings started elsewhere (the app, the button on the feeder), food running low, water running low, desiccant, filter or cleaning due, a low battery, a device going offline or coming back, and settings changed elsewhere (feeding plan, water mode, light). Off by default: every planned meal, since that would be several announcements a day. Feedings, failed feedings, faults, food or water running low, maintenance falling due and a low battery are recorded in the history either way.

Notes on cloud limits

Meross limits its cloud to 200 messages per hour and device – according to Meross support, a safety measure to prevent server flooding. If the limit is exceeded persistently, Meross first sends a warning (a “cloud termination notice”). If the same device still sends too many messages three days after that warning, that device is blocked for 24 hours. Other devices and the account itself are not affected.

The add-on automatically stays below the limit: the periodic polling is deliberately conservative, on/off changes arrive in real time via push anyway, and the add-on additionally caps the requests per device. Should a single device reach the ceiling nonetheless, it is temporarily polled less often; a message names the affected device. All other devices continue normally.

Maturity and notes

Only Netatmo offers an official, documented API. Meross, VeSync and Cozytouch are reverse-engineered cloud integrations without an official API – for Meross, the manufacturer's support has explicitly confirmed on request that no official API is offered. They work reliably with the tested devices but can temporarily fail when the manufacturers change their servers.

The platforms differ in how well they are field-tested:

  • Meross and Netatmo are considered stable.
  • VeSync: Levoit Core series air purifiers, Levoit tower fans and Cosori air fryers of the CAF-P583S series are supported. The air fryer was built against exactly one appliance and is confirmed there; the two-zone TwinFry models speak a different protocol and are not shown. Neither are other VeSync devices (plugs, lights, humidifiers, other kitchen appliances).
  • Cozytouch/Atlantic (experimental): the protocol is unofficial and reverse engineered; Atlantic can change it at any time. Only the Austria Email Revolution Evo 3 hot water heat pump has been tested so far. Other Cozytouch devices (e.g. radiators, air conditioners) are currently incorrectly shown as hot water heat pumps as well and are not usable.
  • Petlibro (experimental): the protocol is unofficial; what the add-on knows about it comes from the community integration for Home Assistant and was checked against a real account. Tested with the Air Smart Feeder (PLAF108) and the Dockstream 2 Cordless water fountain (PLWF116). Petlibro allows one sign-in per account at a time - see Set up Petlibro.

Privacy and security

  • The credentials are stored exclusively locally on the computer. They are not in the NVDA configuration at all, but in a file of their own beside the add-on – which keeps them out of the log as well. Passwords and tokens are encrypted there and can be read back only on the machine and user account they were saved on - since 26.10.1 the email addresses too; older entries in plain text are encrypted at the next save. The file is taken off the inherited access rights when it is written, so other accounts on the same computer cannot read it.
  • When the add-on is removed, it removes the credentials file with it; an update keeps it. The history, the readings, the energy samples and the favourites stay as records of their own - in the add-ons folder as SmartHomeControl_*.json - and can be deleted there by hand.
  • Communication goes directly to the respective manufacturer clouds – no data is sent to third parties.
  • An early version created a section of its own in the NVDA configuration, [merossSmartControl], holding the Netatmo password and client secret in plain text. NVDA writes its configuration into the log at every start, so both stood in every log. The add-on removes this section when it starts. If an NVDA log or nvda.ini from such a machine was ever passed on, the Netatmo password should be changed.
  • Netatmo, VeSync, Cozytouch and Petlibro connect through the network libraries NVDA ships itself (requests, urllib3); their security updates come with NVDA updates. Meross uses a bundled library (aiohttp). The certificates of every platform are checked against the root certificates bundled with the add-on (certifi), not against NVDA's copy. Which versions run is written to the NVDA log after every sign-in.

Troubleshooting

  • No devices visible: check the credentials and the enabled platform in the settings dialog, then save (the sign-in runs in the background). Should the sign-in fail because of the network - NVDA starting before the Wi-Fi, say - the add-on tries again after 30 seconds, after one and after two minutes and every five minutes after that, and announces when the devices are ready. A refused password is not tried again.
  • Device offline: check whether the device is reachable in the manufacturer app.
  • A platform reports "unreachable": usually a temporary cloud/network issue – the add-on reconnects automatically and announces when the platform is connected again. The announcement comes once, and only when the fault has lasted two minutes; the state can be looked up at any time on the platform's line in the device tree ("Netatmo devices (7) - unreachable since 11:30"), in the status line (Ctrl + T) and in the connection diagnostics. Once the values below it have grown noticeably old, the same line also says when they arrived.
  • A platform answers only in part: the Netatmo cloud serves the weather station and the heating separately, and one of them can fail while the other answers normally. This is a state of its own on the platform's line - "weather station not answering" - and is announced once. The values of the failed half stay as they were last read.
  • A single device does not answer: that is not a fault of the platform and is therefore not announced - a slow device timing out on every second poll would otherwise produce a message every few minutes. It can be read in the platform status under "Devices that did not answer"; the device menu keeps the last known values until it answers again.
  • Sensors of a hub are missing: while the hub does not answer, its sensors stay in the list with their last state, marked as unreachable - their favourites keep working. Once the hub answers again they come back on their own, without an NVDA restart.

Platform status

Ctrl + P in the device menu, or the button next to the history. An announcement is gone once it has been spoken; this is where it can be read afterwards. Two views, switched in the "View" box:

  • Events - every state change since signing in, newest first: which platform, when, and what happened. That also answers afterwards whether a heating really was unreachable for half an hour.
  • Devices that did not answer - the devices that missed at least one poll, still failing ones first, with the time of their last answer. A device that answered every poll is not in the list.

Enter on a row opens the detail in a text field that can be read line by line and copied with Ctrl + C. F5 reloads. Both lists cover the running NVDA session: the record is cleared on sign-out, so nothing is reported after a new sign-in that nobody saw fail.

Reporting a problem

Problem reports are welcome – please open an issue at github.com/BlindByte06/SmartHomeControl/issues.

What helps most is the NVDA log. To produce a useful one:

  1. NVDA menu → Preferences → Settings → General → Logging level: "Debug", then restart NVDA. Without this level the add-on's own entries are missing.
  2. Reproduce the problem.
  3. NVDA menu → Tools → View log, then save the text (Ctrl+S) or copy the part around the problem.

Please include in the report:

  • what was done, what was expected and what happened instead
  • the platform and, if known, the device model (e.g. Meross MSS425F)
  • the add-on version (NVDA menu → Tools → Add-on store → installed add-ons) and the NVDA version

Before sending, please check the log for personal data. It contains device and home names as given in the manufacturer app. NVDA also writes the complete configuration into the log at startup. The credentials are no longer part of it: since 26.9.1 email addresses, passwords and tokens live in a file of their own beside the add-on, which the log never sees. The add-on itself logs none of that either, but error messages passed through from the manufacturer libraries are beyond its control, so a quick look is worth it.
